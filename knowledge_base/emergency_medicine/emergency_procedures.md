# Emergency Medicine — Field Procedures
Source: Where There Is No Doctor (Hesperian Foundation) + Army FM 3-05.70
Confidence: HIGH — Field tested globally in low-resource settings

## CRITICAL RULE
You are not a doctor. These procedures are for when no medical help is available.
The goal is to keep the person alive and stable until better care arrives.
When in doubt: keep the person warm, still, hydrated, and calm.
Do less, not more. Unnecessary intervention causes harm.

---

## Triage — Who to Help First

When multiple people are injured, prioritize:

IMMEDIATE (treat now — life threatening but survivable):
- Airway obstruction (not breathing)
- Severe bleeding
- Shock
- Chest wounds with sucking sound

DELAYED (treat next — serious but stable):
- Fractures with pulse below
- Large wounds not actively bleeding
- Burns under 20% body surface

MINIMAL (treat last — walking wounded):
- Minor lacerations
- Sprains, bruises
- Shock that has self-resolved

EXPECTANT (resources better spent elsewhere — in true mass casualty only):
- No pulse, no breathing, not responding
- Brain matter visible
- Burns over 60% body with no airway

## Airway and Breathing

If person is unconscious and not breathing:
1. Tilt head back, lift chin — opens airway
2. Look, listen, feel for breathing — 10 seconds
3. If not breathing: 2 rescue breaths (mouth to mouth)
4. If no pulse: 30 chest compressions, 2 breaths, repeat
5. Compress hard and fast — 5cm deep, 100/minute
6. Do not stop until person breathes, help arrives, or 30 minutes pass

Recovery position (unconscious but breathing):
- Roll onto side
- Top knee bent forward as support
- Head tilted back to maintain airway
- Check breathing every 5 minutes

## Bleeding Control

### Minor Bleeding
Direct pressure with clean cloth — hold 10 minutes without peeking.
Elevation above heart level reduces blood flow.
Do not remove cloth if soaked — add more on top.

### Severe Bleeding (arterial — bright red, spurting)
This is life-threatening. Act immediately.

1. Direct pressure — press as hard as you can
2. Use hand inside wound if necessary — do not be squeamish
3. Maintain pressure 15-20 minutes without releasing
4. Pack wound with clean cloth if available — stuff it in
5. Tourniquet if limb only:

Tourniquet application:
- Use only on arms or legs — NEVER on neck, trunk
- Apply 5-7cm above wound (toward heart)
- Must be at least 4cm wide — narrow materials cause nerve damage
- Improvise from belt, torn cloth, webbing — double layer minimum
- Tighten until bleeding stops — this will hurt — tighten anyway
- Note time of application — write on forehead if necessary
- Do NOT remove in field — removal can cause fatal blood pressure drop
- Tourniquet can stay on up to 2 hours without permanent damage
- Longer: risk of losing limb vs risk of death — usually keep it on

Natural hemostatic agents:
- Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) — pack into wound, very effective styptic
- Cayenne pepper — painful but effective on minor bleeds
- Clean spider web — traditional, has some antimicrobial properties
- Activated charcoal — absorbs moisture, helps clotting
- Honey — antimicrobial, helps wound sealing

## Wound Care and Infection Prevention

### Cleaning a Wound (Most Important Step)
Infection kills more than injury. Clean the wound thoroughly.

1. Wash your hands first with soap and water — non-negotiable
2. Irrigate wound under pressure:
   - Fill plastic bag or bottle with boiled cooled water
   - Poke small hole in corner or use syringe
   - Flush wound with strong stream — more is better
   - Target: 1 liter of fluid through the wound
3. Remove all debris visible — tweezers sterilized in flame
4. Do NOT remove deeply embedded objects — stabilize and leave
5. Apply antiseptic if available:
   - Dilute iodine 1% solution (dilute from tincture)
   - Dilute chlorine solution — 1 drop bleach per liter
   - Honey (raw, unpasteurized — powerful antimicrobial)
   - Do NOT use full-strength hydrogen peroxide — damages healing tissue
6. Cover with clean bandage — change daily or when soiled

### Wound Closure

Do not close:
- Animal bites (seal in infection)
- Puncture wounds (seal in infection)
- Wounds older than 6-8 hours (infection likely present)
- Infected wounds (drainage needed)

Close with butterfly strips:
Cut tape into H-shape — two parallel strips joined by narrow bridge
1. Dry skin thoroughly around wound
2. Apply one arm to one side of wound
3. Pull wound edges firmly together
4. Apply other arm across closed wound
5. Repeat every 3-5mm along entire wound
6. Cover with bandage

Suturing (only when wound won't stay closed otherwise):
- Use finest thread available — sterilize in boiling water 10 minutes
- Smallest curved needle available — sterilize same way
- Technique:
  Enter skin 4mm from wound edge
  Pass through full thickness to same depth other side
  Tie square knot: right over left, pull tight, left over right
  Tails to 5mm length
  Space sutures every 5mm
- Remove sutures: face 3-5 days, body 7-10 days, foot 10-14 days

### Signs of Infection — Monitor Daily
Normal: mild redness, swelling, warmth at wound edges — first 48 hours
Concerning: any of these require aggressive action

- Increasing redness spreading beyond wound edges
- Increasing swelling after day 2
- Pus or thick greenish discharge (clear/pale yellow is normal)
- Wound opens that was previously closed
- Patient develops fever with wound

EMERGENCY — Act immediately:
- Red lines or streaks spreading from wound toward body = blood poisoning starting
- Wound smells rotten or sweet = gangrene possible
- Skin around wound turning black = tissue death
- High fever with confusion + wound present = sepsis

Treatment for infected wound:
- Open wound — remove closure, allow to drain
- Pack open with clean cloth soaked in dilute iodine solution
- Change packing twice daily
- Willow bark tea internally for fever and pain
- Garlic internally — natural antibiotic properties
- If red lines spreading: this is a true emergency — maximum possible treatment

## Fractures and Sprains

### Identifying Fractures vs Sprains
Fracture more likely if:
- Mechanism was violent (fall, crush, direct blow)
- Deformity visible (limb bent wrong way)
- Grinding sensation or sound on movement
- Complete inability to bear weight (lower limb)
- Severe swelling appearing within minutes

### Splinting Principles
Splint in position found — do not try to straighten unless circulation lost.
Splint must immobilize joint above AND joint below fracture.
Pad all bony prominences generously — pressure sores form fast.
Check circulation every 1 hour after splinting:
- Capillary refill: press fingernail, release, should pink in under 2 seconds
- Sensation: can patient feel touch below fracture?
- Pulse: present below fracture?
- Movement: can patient move fingers or toes?

Improvised splints:
- Straight branches padded with folded cloth
- Rolled sleeping mat or foam
- Firm cardboard folded and padded
- Fractured leg: buddy-splint to uninjured leg
- Fractured arm: sling against body provides splinting

### Reducing a Closed Fracture
Only attempt if:
- Circulation is lost below fracture AND cannot be restored by repositioning
- You are more than several days from any help
- Patient consents and understands risks

Method:
1. Explain to patient — this will hurt
2. Give any available pain relief first — wait 20 minutes
3. Apply firm, steady, gentle traction along axis of bone
4. Assistant holds limb above fracture while you apply traction below
5. Continue traction steadily while gently moving toward normal alignment
6. Slight grinding is normal — do not stop for this
7. Stop if patient screams or resistance suddenly increases
8. Splint immediately in position achieved — do not release traction until splinted
9. Recheck circulation after — if worse, re-splint in position found

## Burns

### Immediate Treatment
1. Remove from heat source — stop the burning
2. Remove clothing and jewelry near burn — do not pull off if stuck to skin
3. Cool with running cool (not cold) water for 20 minutes
4. Do not use ice — causes additional tissue damage
5. Do not apply butter, oil, or toothpaste — seals in heat, causes infection
6. Cover with clean damp cloth — keep moist

### Assessing Burn Severity

First degree (superficial):
- Red, painful, no blisters
- Treat: cool water, aloe vera, keep clean
- Heals in 3-7 days

Second degree (partial thickness):
- Blisters, very painful, wet-looking
- Do NOT break blisters — they protect from infection
- Treat: keep covered and moist, change dressing daily
- Honey dressing excellent for burns — antimicrobial and moist
- Heals in 2-3 weeks

Third degree (full thickness):
- White, brown, or black — leathery appearance
- Painless in burned area (nerves destroyed) but surrounding area painful
- Requires major medical intervention — focus on infection prevention
- Keep clean, cover, manage infection, nutrition critical

### Rule of Nines — Estimating Burn Area
Head and neck: 9%
Each arm: 9%
Chest front: 9%
Chest back: 9%
Abdomen front: 9%
Abdomen back: 9%
Each thigh: 9%
Each lower leg: 9%
Groin: 1%

Burns requiring maximum care:
- Over 20% body surface area (any severity)
- Over 10% body surface area (third degree)
- Burns to face, hands, feet, genitals, joints
- Inhalation burns (singed eyebrows, hoarse voice, soot in mouth)

Inhalation burns: highest priority
Airway can swell shut within hours
Keep patient sitting upright
Cool mist on face if possible
Prepare for airway to close — know how to manage

## Shock

Shock = circulatory system failure — blood not reaching vital organs
Kills quickly if not treated. Recognize early.

Signs of shock:
- Pale, cold, clammy skin
- Rapid weak pulse (over 100 beats per minute)
- Rapid breathing
- Confusion or anxiety
- Extreme thirst
- Nausea

Treatment:
1. Treat the cause if identifiable (stop bleeding, treat burns)
2. Lay patient flat
3. Elevate legs 30cm if no spinal injury suspected
4. Keep warm — cold worsens shock
5. Do NOT give food or fluid if patient may need surgery
6. If conscious and no abdominal injury: oral rehydration solution
   Mix: 1 liter boiled water, 6 teaspoons sugar, 1/2 teaspoon salt
   Sip slowly — not gulps
7. Reassure and calm — anxiety worsens shock
8. Monitor breathing and pulse continuously

## Childbirth Without Medical Assistance

Most births are uncomplicated. The body knows what to do.
Your role: create clean safe space, do not interfere unnecessarily.

Preparation:
- Clean hands thoroughly
- Boil string or cord for tying umbilical cord
- Boil scissors or blade for cutting
- Clean cloths and blankets
- Warm water ready

Normal labor — three stages:

Stage 1 (hours to day):
- Contractions begin, gradually closer together
- Cervix dilates — you cannot check this without internal exam
- Keep mother comfortable, walking helps
- Encourage rest between contractions
- Light food and fluid if desired
- Labor is not an emergency — do not rush it

Stage 2 (minutes to hours — pushing):
- Mother feels strong urge to push with each contraction
- Do not tell her to push harder — her body knows
- Between pushes: rest completely
- Baby's head appears (crowning) — do not pull
- Support perineum with gentle hand pressure to slow delivery and prevent tearing
- Once head is out: check for cord around neck — if present, loop over baby's head gently
- Body usually follows next contraction — continue to support, do not pull

Stage 3 (up to 1 hour — placenta):
- After baby is born, cord stops pulsating in 2-3 minutes
- Tie cord in two places 10cm and 15cm from baby's belly
- Cut between ties with sterilized blade
- Placenta delivers naturally — do not pull cord
- Gentle pushing may help
- Some bleeding is normal — up to 500ml
- More than 500ml or bleeding that won't slow: massage uterus firmly

Newborn care:
- Dry immediately — cold is dangerous
- Clear mouth and nose with finger or cloth if not breathing
- Stimulate if not crying: rub back firmly, flick soles of feet
- Skin-to-skin contact immediately — best warmth and bonding
- Breast milk begins in 2-3 days — colostrum (clear/yellow) comes first and is vital

When to be very concerned:
- Cord comes before baby (prolapsed cord) — emergency — keep cord moist, get to help
- Baby's leg or arm appears first instead of head — breech is manageable, arm is emergency
- Bleeding that doesn't slow after placenta delivered
- Baby not breathing after 5 minutes of stimulation

## Improvised Medicines

### Willow Bark — Natural Aspirin
Active compound: salicin (converts to salicylic acid in body)
Uses: Pain, fever, inflammation — same mechanism as aspirin

Preparation:
- Use bark of white willow, black willow, or any willow species
- Inner bark is most potent (scrape outer bark away)
- Simmer 1 tablespoon bark per cup water for 20 minutes
- Strain and drink 1 cup up to 4 times per day
- Tastes bitter — honey helps
- Do not give to children under 12 (same Reye's syndrome concern as aspirin)

Dosage note: potency varies — start conservative and adjust

### Garlic — Broad Spectrum Antimicrobial
Uses: Bacterial infections, fungal infections, wound treatment

Preparation for internal use:
- Crush 2-4 cloves, let sit 10 minutes (activates allicin)
- Eat raw or mix with honey
- Continue 3 times daily for infections
- Strong smell on breath and skin is expected

Preparation for wounds:
- Crush, mix with small amount of water or oil
- Apply to wound surface
- Can cause skin irritation — dilute if burning is severe

### Honey — Wound Dressing
Raw, unprocessed honey is powerfully antimicrobial.
Effective against MRSA and other antibiotic-resistant bacteria.
Creates moist wound environment ideal for healing.

Application:
- Apply thick layer directly to wound
- Cover with clean bandage
- Change every 24-48 hours
- Wound should show improvement within 3-4 days
- If not improving: increase honey, or suspect deeper infection

Note: Pasteurized commercial honey has reduced but still useful antimicrobial properties.
Manuka honey (from New Zealand) has highest proven antimicrobial activity.
Local raw honey from any region has significant antimicrobial properties.

### Turmeric — Anti-inflammatory and Wound Paste
Uses: Inflammation, wounds, infections

Internal: 1 teaspoon powder in warm water or milk, 3 times daily
Wound paste: Mix turmeric powder with enough water to form thick paste, apply to wound, cover
Stains everything yellow permanently — be aware

### Plantain (Plantago major) — Drawing Poultice
Common garden weed found globally. Large oval ribbed leaves.
Not the banana — the common lawn weed.

Uses: Insect stings, minor wounds, drawing out infection, splinters

Preparation:
- Clean fresh leaf
- Chew or crush until juicy
- Apply directly to wound or sting
- Hold in place with bandage
- Change every few hours

Oral rehydration solution — the life-saver:
Used for diarrhea, vomiting, heat exhaustion, mild shock.
More lives saved globally than any other single intervention.

Recipe (per liter of boiled cooled water):
- 6 level teaspoons sugar (sucrose)
- 1/2 level teaspoon salt (sodium chloride)
- Optional: 1/2 cup orange juice or half banana for potassium

Mix thoroughly. Taste should be no saltier than tears.
Give in sips — adults up to 1 liter per hour if severe dehydration
Children: 100ml per kg body weight over 4 hours for severe dehydration
