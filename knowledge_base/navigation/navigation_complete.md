# Navigation Without GPS — Complete Field Guide
Source: Army FM 3-05.70 + Historical Navigation Methods
Confidence: HIGH — Proven methods used for millennia

## Core Principle
You are never truly lost if you can determine:
1. Which direction is north
2. What terrain features surround you
3. Where water flows (always toward civilization or coast)

Learn these methods in this order — from most reliable to most situational.

---

## Finding North — Definitive Methods

### Method 1: Polaris (North Star) — Most Reliable at Night

The North Star (Polaris) sits almost exactly above the North Pole.
It appears stationary while all other stars rotate around it.
Pointing at Polaris means pointing at True North.

Finding Polaris:
1. Find Ursa Major (the Big Dipper / Plough) — 7 bright stars in dipper shape
2. Locate the two stars forming the outer edge of the dipper cup
3. Draw an imaginary line through these two stars extending upward
4. Travel along this line approximately 5 times the distance between the two stars
5. The moderately bright star at that point is Polaris

Confirming Polaris:
- It does not move — watch for 15 minutes, all other stars rotate, Polaris stays fixed
- Ursa Minor (Little Dipper) — Polaris is the tip of its handle
- Cassiopeia (W or M shape) — Polaris is on opposite side from Big Dipper, equidistant

Determining your latitude from Polaris:
- Measure the angle of Polaris above the horizon
- This angle equals your latitude in degrees North
- Hand method: fist held at arm's length covers approximately 10 degrees
- Stack fists from horizon to Polaris — count fists × 10 = rough latitude

This method works in ALL weather where stars are visible.
In Southern Hemisphere: Polaris is not visible. Use Southern Cross instead.

### Southern Cross (Southern Hemisphere)
1. Find the Southern Cross — 4 bright stars in cross shape
2. Extend the long axis of the cross 4.5 times its length
3. That point in the sky is above True South
4. Drop a perpendicular line from that sky-point to the horizon — that is South

---

### Method 2: Shadow Stick — Most Reliable in Daytime

Works anywhere in the world. Requires only a stick and patience.
Results are accurate to within a few degrees.

1. Find flat ground. Push straight stick vertically into ground.
2. Mark the tip of the shadow with a stone or scratch — this is Point A
3. Wait 15-30 minutes minimum. Mark new shadow tip — this is Point B.
4. Draw a line from Point A to Point B.
5. This line runs WEST (A) to EAST (B) — regardless of hemisphere or time of year.
6. Stand with left foot at A and right foot at B — you are now facing North.

Why it works: the sun always moves from east to west.
Shadow tip always moves from west to east.
This is geometry, not assumption — it is always correct.

Longer wait = more accurate result. 30+ minutes is better than 15.

---

### Method 3: Watch Method — Quick but Less Accurate

Traditional watch (with hour hand) in Northern Hemisphere:
1. Hold watch horizontal
2. Point hour hand toward the sun
3. Bisect the angle between the hour hand and 12 o'clock
4. That bisecting line points South
5. Opposite direction is North

Southern Hemisphere:
Point 12 o'clock toward the sun.
Bisect angle between 12 and hour hand. This points North.

Accuracy: Good within 10-15 degrees. Better near equinoxes (spring/autumn).
Less reliable within 30 degrees of equator.
Requires actual analog watch face.

---

### Method 4: Sun Direction by Time

Simple rules — less precise but instantly useful:
- Northern Hemisphere: Sun rises roughly East, sets roughly West, and at noon is due South
- Southern Hemisphere: Sun rises roughly East, sets roughly West, and at noon is due North
- Equator: Sun passes directly overhead (or very close) at noon

Sun direction throughout day (Northern Hemisphere):
- 6am: East
- 9am: Southeast
- Noon: South
- 3pm: Southwest
- 6pm: West

### Method 5: Natural Indicators — Useful Secondary Confirmation

These are confirmatory clues, not primary navigation tools. Use to confirm, not to lead.

Tree growth patterns:
- Moss grows heaviest on North side of trees (Northern Hemisphere) — gets less sun
- Tree rings are wider on South side — more sun = more growth
- Trees often lean slightly South toward maximum sunlight
- Bark on South side often lighter in color

Snow and ice:
- Snow melts fastest on South-facing slopes (Northern Hemisphere)
- South-facing slopes have less snow coverage in winter
- Ice on North-facing slopes lasts longer into spring

Prevailing winds:
- Mid-latitudes (30-60°N or S): prevailing winds generally from West
- Equatorial regions: trade winds from East
- Coastal: daytime wind comes from sea (onshore), night from land (offshore)
- Know your region's prevailing wind — local knowledge is gold

Vegetation density:
- North-facing slopes (NH): dense undergrowth, ferns, shade-tolerant plants
- South-facing slopes (NH): more open, grasses, sun-tolerant shrubs, drier

## Determining Latitude

### With Polaris (Most Accurate)
Angle of Polaris above horizon = your latitude in degrees North
Method: hold protractor at eye level aligned to horizon, measure angle to Polaris
Improvised: use clinometer made from any angle-measuring device

### From Sun at Noon
Requires knowing the date.

1. Find true solar noon (when shadow is shortest — watch throughout day)
2. Measure sun's angle above horizon at that exact moment
3. Calculate:
   - At equinoxes (March 20, Sept 22): latitude = 90 - noon sun angle
   - At summer solstice (June 21, NH): latitude = 90 - noon sun angle + 23.5
   - At winter solstice (Dec 21, NH): latitude = 90 - noon sun angle - 23.5

Example: Noon sun angle = 50° on March 20
Latitude = 90 - 50 = 40° North

---

## Determining Longitude

Longitude requires knowing exact current time (UTC/GMT).
Without a clock, longitude cannot be determined precisely.
With a clock showing UTC time:

At solar noon (when shadow shortest):
Longitude = (12:00 UTC - local solar noon time) × 15 degrees per hour

Example: Solar noon occurs at 14:00 UTC
Longitude = (12 - 14) × 15 = -30° = 30° West

## Terrain Navigation

### Reading Watersheds
Water always flows from high to low ground.
Following streams always leads to larger rivers, then sea.
Larger rivers usually have human settlements.
If you need to find people: follow water downstream.
If you need high ground for signaling: follow water upstream.

River navigation rules:
- Left bank and right bank defined from perspective of facing downstream
- Rivers cut valleys — always in lowest terrain
- Rivers braid on flat ground (multiple channels)
- Rivers cut single deep channels in steep terrain

### Contour Reading Without a Map
Even without a topographic map, terrain tells a story:
- V-shaped valleys: young rivers, steep sides, fast water
- U-shaped valleys: glaciated terrain, flat valley floor, good farming potential
- Ridge lines: excellent navigation landmarks, visible from distance
- Saddles (low point between two peaks): easiest ridge crossing point
- Cliffs: water collects at base, often springs present

### Finding Your Elevation
Without instruments: observe vegetation zones
- Tropical lowland forest: near sea level
- Cloud forest: typically 1500-2500m
- Alpine meadow: typically 2500-3500m
- Tree line: typically 3000-4000m (varies by latitude)
- Permanent snow line: varies from 2000m (polar) to 5000m (tropical)

## Navigation Without a Map — Dead Reckoning

When you have no map but know roughly where you started:

1. Establish your starting point
2. Choose a bearing (direction) toward your target
3. Walk in that direction, maintaining bearing using compass or sun
4. Track distance: average walking pace = 1km per 12-15 minutes on flat ground
5. Account for terrain: steep uphill = double time, rough ground = 50% more time
6. Record: direction walked + time walked
7. Calculate position periodically

Improvised compass:
- Magnetize sewing needle by stroking with magnet (one direction only, 50+ times)
- Float needle on leaf in still water
- Needle aligns North-South (test against Polaris to determine which end is North)
- Or suspend from thread — hangs and rotates freely to North

## World Landmarks for Location Estimation

If you can identify terrain features and match to mental map:

Mountain ranges visible from hundreds of kilometers:
- Alps: Central Europe, roughly East-West
- Himalayas: South Asia, roughly East-West, highest on Earth
- Rockies: Western North America, roughly North-South
- Andes: Western South America, roughly North-South, runs full length
- Atlas: Northwest Africa
- Urals: Russia, divides Europe from Asia, roughly North-South
- Great Dividing Range: Eastern Australia, roughly North-South

Major rivers:
- Amazon: South America, flows East, widest river on Earth
- Nile: Africa, flows North, longest river
- Mississippi: North America, flows South
- Yangtze: China, flows East
- Rhine/Danube: Europe, flow East into Black Sea
- Congo: Central Africa, flows West

Deserts (useful negative indicator — if in desert, you know region):
- Sahara: North Africa, largest hot desert
- Arabian: Middle East
- Gobi: Central Asia (China/Mongolia)
- Atacama: West South America, driest on Earth
- Australian interior: mostly arid
- Sonoran/Mojave: Southwestern North America

Identifying biome gives regional placement:
- Tropical rainforest: within 10° of equator
- Savanna/grassland: 10-20° from equator
- Desert: 20-30° from equator (or rain shadow)
- Mediterranean: 30-40° (wet winters, dry summers)
- Temperate: 40-60° from equator
- Boreal/Taiga: 50-70° from equator
- Tundra: above 70° or above tree line

## Signaling for Rescue

If you want to be found:

Universal distress signal: 3 of anything — 3 fires, 3 whistle blasts, 3 shots
International Morse code SOS: ... --- ... (3 short, 3 long, 3 short)

Signal fire:
- Build in open location visible from air
- Smoke visible in daytime: add green vegetation, rubber, plastic to produce dark smoke
- Light visible at night: bright dry fire without smoke
- Signal fire triangle: three fires 25-30m apart (classic rescue signal)

Ground signals (visible from aircraft):
- X = need medical assistance (largest symbol possible)
- V = need help
- → = traveling this direction
- LL = all is well
- Make symbols minimum 10m tall — use rocks, logs, trampled vegetation, anything contrasting

Mirror signaling:
- Glass, polished metal, any reflective surface
- Flash aimed at aircraft or distant point can be seen 50+ km
- Aim: hold mirror near eye, extend other hand toward target, flash mirror to hit hand
- Creates visible flash visible at extreme range
