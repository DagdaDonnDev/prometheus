## Conservation of Mass in Reactions

Atoms are rearranged in a chemical reaction, never created or destroyed.
A balanced equation has the same number of each kind of atom on both sides.

## The Mole

One mole is 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number, N_A).
The molar mass of a substance in grams per mole equals its formula mass.

    moles n = mass m / molar mass M

Common atomic masses (g/mol): H 1.008, C 12.01, N 14.01, O 16.00, Na 22.99,
Mg 24.31, Al 26.98, S 32.06, Cl 35.45, K 39.10, Ca 40.08, Fe 55.85, Cu 63.55.

Examples: water H₂O = 18.02 g/mol. Sodium hydroxide NaOH = 40.00 g/mol.
Potassium hydroxide KOH = 56.11 g/mol. Carbon dioxide CO₂ = 44.01 g/mol.

## Stoichiometry

The coefficients in a balanced equation give the mole ratio in which substances react.
To find the mass of product: convert mass of reactant to moles, apply the mole ratio,
convert back to mass.

The limiting reagent is the reactant that runs out first. It sets the maximum yield.

## Concentration and Dilution

    Concentration c = n / V  (mol per litre, mol/L)
    Dilution: c1 V1 = c2 V2
    Always add acid or lye to water, never water to acid or lye — the heat released
    can make water boil and spatter.

## Ideal Gas Law

    P V = n R T
    R = 8.314 J/(mol·K). T must be in kelvin (K = °C + 273.15).
    At 0 °C and 1 atm, one mole of an ideal gas occupies about 22.4 litres.

## Acids and Bases

pH = −log10[H⁺]. pH 7 is neutral at 25 °C. Below 7 is acidic, above 7 is alkaline.
Each pH unit is a factor of ten in hydrogen-ion concentration.
Acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation).

## Saponification

Fat (triglyceride) + strong alkali → soap + glycerol.
Sodium hydroxide gives hard soap. Potassium hydroxide (from wood-ash lye) gives soft soap.

## Oxidation and Reduction

Oxidation is loss of electrons. Reduction is gain of electrons. They always occur together.
Combustion is rapid oxidation: fuel + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + heat.
Smelting reduces metal ores: iron oxide + carbon monoxide → iron + carbon dioxide.

## Electrochemistry

In a galvanic cell, the more reactive metal is the anode (oxidised) and the less reactive
is the cathode. Cell voltage depends on the pair of metals and the electrolyte.
Reactivity series (most to least reactive): K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Ag, Au.

Faraday's law of electrolysis: mass deposited m = (Q × M) / (z × F),
where Q is charge in coulombs, z is electrons per ion, F = 96,485 C/mol.

## Energy in Reactions

Exothermic reactions release heat (combustion, neutralisation, slaking lime).
Endothermic reactions absorb heat (dissolving ammonium nitrate, thermal decomposition
of limestone: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ above about 840 °C).
